# Native UI patterns

Every new or changed view in the Founderizer app follows these rules. The references are the owner's Codex Settings screenshots: a large page title, a page sidebar with icons in place of tabs, soft section cards, a row with a symbol, a title and a short detail at the left and one control at the right, a white main button, a tinted Delete button, a centered empty state, and a sub-page with Back in place of a modal. The app has no horizontal tab bar: a page with sections has its own sidebar (`PageSidebar`), and a filter is a `FleetSelect`. The Apple guidance agrees: a grouped form puts labels at the leading edge and controls at the trailing edge, and settings stay few. The components are in [`FormRows.swift`](Sources/Founderizer/FormRows.swift), [`FleetSelect.swift`](Sources/Founderizer/FleetSelect.swift), [`AgentVisuals.swift`](Sources/Founderizer/AgentVisuals.swift) and [`AgentGlyph.swift`](Sources/Founderizer/AgentGlyph.swift). An agent's mark itself — its shape, its colour and the rule that gives a name its own — is [`FleetMark`](Sources/FleetMark/AgentMark.swift), which this app and the owner's iPhone app both link, so one agent wears one face on his two screens.

## One page, one scaffold

**Every screen of the app is a `FleetPage`** (`FormRows.swift`), and the scaffold holds the whole standard so that a
page cannot be built another way. It gives the page one column of one width (1,100 pt, `FleetStyle.pageWidth`) with
one pair of margins (24 pt), the scroll — in which every block keeps its own height (the polish of 30 October 2026: a long page cut a heading with a detail line back to 28 pt and gave the room to a text box) — and one header. **Boards and tables take 1,100 pt, and the Tasks board the window's whole width (`wide:`, below). A form page's header and
cards take 680 pt from the same left edge** (`FormMetrics.formWidth`; the owner's yes of 28 October 2026, D-8), with
the free room at their right: the title where every page has it, the header's actions ending where the cards end (the
owner, 5 November 2026, of Add URL and Add file 420 pt past the cards on Settings › Knowledge: "either the content
needs to be wider or the top buttons moved"), and a `FormSection` — its heading, its card and its footer. A page is a
form unless it says `board:` (a table) or `fills:` (the Tasks board, and Needs you, whose lane and request at hand
stand in the page's column).
The header: the title once, at 26 pt medium, with the page's actions at
its right — at most one white main button, and every other action an outline capsule. **A page has no subtitle**, and
the scaffold has nowhere to put one: a page says what it is by its name and by what it shows, so no line under a
title explains the page, and no sentence under a section says what the section is. Under the header come the page's
blocks, 22 pt apart (`FleetSpace.block`), each one a `FormSection` card. 28 pt stand over the title and 24 pt under
the last block (`FleetSpace.top`, `.bottom`). A detail page puts a `FleetBack` in the band over its title, at the title's x (`FleetSpace.back`), so its title and
its buttons stand where every page has them; Esc also follows it, and it is the one back link of a page. A setup's wizard has its Back at the top left of its page instead (`SetupPage`'s Back, `SetupBack`: a left chevron and Back, muted, as `FleetBack` draws a page's back link, at the step's size; his yes of the same day to the arrow), at one place on every setup page and still when a step scrolls (the owner, 10 November 2026: "I want it to be always in the same position during all screens of the onboarding"); and a page that asks for a key, a token or a code made on another site shows what to do there as short numbered steps over its field (`SetupSteps`), the first with that page's link, and no "Open …" link under the field (the same day: "show simple steps ABOVE the input because thye should be done BEFORE pastign the key ... this applies to everything!").
A link in those steps reads as a link: its words underlined, then a no-break space and the arrow ↗, all in `FleetStyle.link`, and the arrow is part of the link, so a click on it opens the page too (`SetupSteps.words`; the owner, 12 November 2026, of "Open Keys in Tailscale": "make it obvious that Keys is a link, add an icon after it and underline the word too").
A page's one action belongs in the header and nowhere else: an empty
state says that there is nothing yet and offers no button of its own, because the header already has it. Nothing in
the scaffold can be set from outside, apart from `fills:`, which the Tasks board takes because its lanes are as
high as the window and scroll by themselves, `board:` for a table, and `wide:`, which only the Tasks board takes: **its
page is the window's whole width in the same 24 pt margins, and its three lanes share it** (the owner, 2 November 2026,
of the board in the 1,100-pt column in the middle of his 2,560-pt window: "this page should be full width"). A window
too narrow for three lanes of 150 pt shows as many whole lanes as fit, and scrolls one lane at a time.

**Adding is one shape, in one of two places** (the owner, 24 October 2026, with Servers' Add server beside the MCPs
page's "Add an MCP server" row: "review the app for inconsistencies across screens"). A page's add is the white
button of its header, its words with a plus — `Label("Add server", systemImage: "plus")`: Add server, Add secret, Add
file, Add person. A group of a page that adds on its own puts its button on its own heading, as an outline capsule
(Add rule, Connect account, Add device, Add MCP server). **An agent's own lists show the fleet's rows with that
agent's share; each kind is brought into the fleet in its one Settings section** (the owner's yes of 28 October 2026
to D-5, one editor for each kind; on 25 October 2026 he had asked for "the same format between them AND also the same
format as the main Settings"): an agent's Secrets, Knowledge and Apps — with their MCP servers — are groups of its Can
use, and each row opens the same card as Settings. **On the agent's own page each group is managed from that agent's
end** (the owner, 3 November 2026: "we should be able to MANAGE the existing ones for the Agent from its own screen
too", and his yes to "a + on each heading and a − on each row"): the heading's `+` (an `IconButton`-look trigger of a
`FleetSelect`) lists what the fleet already holds of that kind and the agent does not, and one choice gives it at the
kind's lowest level with the write its card makes; each row's `−` takes it back from that agent alone; a row the agent
holds from its profile (a secret its profile lists) has no control at its end: its profile changes that, and its card says
so. Only a refusal leaves a line under a group, in the hub's words; a change that worked shows in the list itself. The `+` shows only while
there is one to give and the hub answers. A refusal is one line under that group's card, in the hub's words. **GitHub's
`+` opens the list of every repository that the fleet's GitHub Apps reach** (`RepositoryPicker.swift`, hub feature
`repo_picker`; the owner's yes of 9 November 2026 to the list, and of 11 November 2026 to its place and its picture:
"Yes, under the heading"): a repository that no agent holds yet is in no list of what the fleet holds, so the menu could
not give it. This `+` is there while the hub answers, whatever is left to give: what the GitHub Apps reach is read only
when the list opens. The list is a card under the heading, over the agent's own rows: a search field (each word in a
repository's name without its owner; "No repository matches the search." as Knowledge says it of its filter); each
account as GitHub writes it, a `FormRow` with "N of M" (what the agent has or gets of its repositories) and a tick for
all of them that the search shows; each repository in the row's 13 with its tick at the right, ticked and dimmed where
the agent has it, and an account's tick too when it has every one; Read or Write (`FleetSegmented`) for all of them;
Cancel and the white Add N, where N counts only what the agent does not have. The repositories scroll in the card past
360 pt. Add sends one write (`POST /ui/team/repos` with `agent` and `level`, against the version that Can use read) of
only what the agent does not hold yet, since the hub sets each repository it names to the level; on 409 it reads again
and sends what is still not held, once more. The `+` waits as a spinner meanwhile and is dimmed while the card is open.
The card and its ticks stay, and wait, until the hub has taken the write, then the card closes and the new rows show in the
agent's list; a refusal, or a hub that did not answer, is one orange line in the card, and it is the line under the
agent's rows when the card was closed meanwhile. A hub without the feature keeps the menu (`AccessGive`). **An agent's
repositories of one account at one level are one row once there are more than three** (`AccessSettings.repositoryRows`,
`Integrations.groups`; the owner's yes of 9 November 2026 to "AbcCorp 179 Read" as one row, and of 11 November 2026
to its picture on ABC Developer's Can use: "Yes, as shown"): the repository's tile, the account as GitHub writes it, its
count and its level in the muted detail, and a chevron in the column of the rows' −. A click opens it to the
repositories' own rows, each with its card and its −, and closes it again. Three or fewer keep their own rows. The rows
go by account, then from Read to Deploy. The list of what the GitHub Apps reach is read at launch, on its own
(`RepositoryPicking.prefetch`), so the account stands as GitHub writes it from the row's first frame. A group
with nothing in it shows the empty card (`FormEmpty`: "No secrets", "No files", "No repositories", "No accounts", "No
MCP servers"), never its heading alone (the owner: "it is weird to show the categories there like empty"). An add of
the fleet's is never a row of a card ("Add an MCP server · Add", "Connect an account · Connect", "Another file · Add"),
and never a `+` on a line of its own over a card.

A view that is a **section of a page** — `AccessSettings`, `AppsSettings`, `AgentMemorySection`, `DevicesSection`, and
`SecretsPage`, `KnowledgePage`, `PeoplePage`, `ConversationsPage` and `ChannelsPage` with `grouped` — brings no header,
no column and no scroll: the page around it brings all three, and a section that set a width of its own is how one
screen came out with four left edges. An agent's four parts are pages of their own (`AgentTab.ownsPage`), and each
part's groups are such sections.

The app has **one second width**, and it is not a page's: a conversation and a task thread stand in 780 pt
(`chatColumn()`, `FleetStyle.readingWidth`), because there the measure is the length of a line of prose and 1,100
points is too long to read. No page may take it.

A conversation also takes **no card**. An agent's chat and a task thread are bare rows on the window's own
background — the agent's words as plain text, the owner's own message in a bubble, a thread's rows with a mark at
the left — and neither screen is a `FleetPage`. The cards inside them are forms that ask the owner something: in a
task thread, the one that asks him to confirm a result that nobody knows ("Check the result"); in an agent's chat,
**the requests that agent asked him** (`NeedRequestCard`; the owner's yes of 28 October 2026, D-7): each stands right
after the agent's message that told him about it ("It is in Needs you."), so he answers in the chat where the agent told
him, and Needs you has them all in its lane; one he answered stays in the chat, folded to two lines. Each is a card because it is a form, and not
because it stands in a conversation. The plan of a task that runs is no card either: it stands open under the thread's
latest activity in the box of an open fold, with no fold over it, and goes when the task ends (`LivePlan`; the owner's
yes of 4 November 2026 to its mockup).

**The flat shape is the owner's own decision of 20 October 2026, and no longer an inherited one.** His fleet had
never run a task, so he had never seen a task thread until a check build put one on his screen. He took it for a
fault ("this is super broken now!?"; "not loading the proper styles"), and the styles were loading: the two files
that draw a thread were unchanged since the build on his Mac. Shown his agent's chat and a task thread side by
side, he kept them both as they are. It is one decision for both, because they are one set of views —
`ThreadViews.swift`, `ChatStyle`, `chatColumn()` — so a frame around one is a frame around the other. To put a
conversation in a card is to reopen this, and it is his to reopen.

**A conversation that he only reads is a chat too** (`ReadChat`; the owner, 6 November 2026: "it needs to look like a
chat", and his yes to its mockup): a conversation of an agent's People, with a person or with another agent. The agent
of the page stands at the right in the fill of his own bubble, the other side at the left in its outline — two looks,
because the filled bubble of an agent's own chat is always his words — with the speaker's name over each run, the
chat's day and time in the middle (`ChatStamp`), the newest at the bottom where it opens, and the time and Copy of a
message on hover beside its bubble. What an agent asked him in a conversation with a person, and his answer, stand in
the middle in the stamps' look, because the person never sees them. It has no composer. Its name and back link stand
where a page has them and stay over the scroll (`ReadChatPage`), and the chat stands in the column of prose.

Take every measure from `FleetSpace` and `FormMetrics`, never a number of your own: a number written into one page is
a number that the next page will not have, which is how the app lost its consistency the first time.

## Appearance

- Use the `FleetStyle` colors in `Components.swift` and the sizes in `FormMetrics`. Do not add colors. The app is dark only.
- Sizes (T-23): **every word takes a token, and no view writes a size of its own.** A page shows three sizes and two weights (`FormMetrics`): its title 26 pt medium (`pageTitle`); a row's title, a section's heading, a card's title, the head of a page's own sidebar ("Settings", an agent's name) and a plan's name 13 pt medium (`title`, `heading`); a row's words, a value, a field's words and the words of a page that set no font 13 pt (`text`, `FleetStyle.body`), and a button's 13 pt medium (Buttons, below); a detail, a footer, a time, a T-number, a table's small words and a count 12 pt (`detail`), and the head of a few details 12 pt medium (`detailTitle`); code 12 pt monospaced (`code`), and a name in code on a row (a secret's variable) the row's size, monospaced; a key or a token that he pastes in its field's own words, never monospaced (the owner, 10 November 2026: no "console" font where he pastes a key, as a two-factor code screen shows its code in its product's own typeface); a code he types elsewhere (a provider's sign-in code, a phone's pairing code) at the title's 26 (`oneTimeCode`), as a setup step shows Codex's code at its own title's 22, and a chat app's link message, which he copies or sends from its QR code, at a row title's 13, all in the app's own typeface: no key, token or code that he pastes, types or reads is monospaced (the owner, 10 November 2026), and only an address, a command and code keep the monospaced font. Nothing is under 12 pt. A chat adds its 14 (below), and a setup step keeps its 22 and 15 (`SetupMetrics`, below). An SF Symbol keeps the size it is drawn at, from `IconMetrics.symbol`, so `.system(size:` stands only in these token places. An agent's chat and a task thread use `ChatStyle`, from the owner's reference (the Claude desktop chat, measured on 16 October 2026): SF Pro 14 pt with lines 22.5 pt apart (6 pt line spacing); a centred 12 pt muted stamp, "Today 15:06" or "Tuesday 10:56", over the first message of a chat and over each message that comes an hour or a day after the one before (`ChatStamp`, the phone's rule; D-7), and a message's own time in its footer under the pointer; a reply 28–30 pt under his message, his picture included, as his next message stands under the reply, and coming in one 0.2-s cross-fade with the waiting line it replaces, so no frame shows neither (`ReplyArrival`, T-20); **the waiting line's "Thinking" has the agent's own glyph before it, 16 pt with no dot, moving as it does at work** (`ChatWaitingLine`; the owner, 13 November 2026: "On the conversation itself, the Thinking label should have the agent icon animating too"), and a wait that is not the agent's work keeps its orange mark and no glyph; a chat's and a task thread's rows keep no room under their last row (`threadRows`), so they stand at the same place over the composer whether they opened at their end or followed a new row there (T-06); 21 pt between blocks, which gives the reference's 37.5 pt from the last line of a block to the first line of the next (SwiftUI adds line spacing only inside one text); 10 pt between two items of one list; task thread rows 20 pt apart, with an icon and the text in white 0.70; the owner's bubble in white 0.24 with 16 pt corners and 16 × 12 pt padding; times, footers and small labels in 12 pt; code at its one size in a chat, 12.5 pt monospaced (`ChatStyle.code`); a request's title 14 pt medium (`ChatStyle.title`). `MessageText(text:chat:)` and `FoldRow(… chat:)` take the chat measures; forms, goals, schedules and knowledge files keep `FleetStyle.body`.
- Shapes: every button is a capsule (see Buttons below), and a screen has three corner radii besides capsules and circles (`FormMetrics`): 13 for a card (a section, a choice, the card over the page), 8 for what stands in a card or is chosen in a list (a row's fill, a field, a code block, an answer), and 16 for the owner's own message (`ChatStyle.bubbleRadius`). The composer of a chat and a task's guidance are a capsule: its corners are half its 40 points at rest (`ComposerCapsule`); a request's answer box keeps its 20.
- Colors: titles `FleetStyle.text`; details and values `FleetStyle.muted`; warnings and errors `.orange`; storage errors `.red` (`FleetNotices`). Numbers and labels use the text colors; a mark beside them carries a state color. **Violet is what he can press, and the brand's mark. A state is never violet: working is green, needs you orange, failed red** (the owner's yes of 28 October 2026, D-8): violet is a link, the focus ring, the selected tab and the mark — a link's words in the lighter violet `FleetStyle.link` (#8F88FF, 5.4:1 on a card; fix round 2: the accent read 3.4:1, and words ask 4.5:1); a count is a grey pill (`SidebarBadge`), an answer option of a request a neutral grey row (`NeedOptionButtonStyle`), a delivery's read checks and a kept file are muted, and a chat's ready Send wears the colour of the agent it writes to (D-6) while every other ready Send — a Needs-you answer, a task's guidance — is violet (fix round 2). The one violet dot is the unread dot of a new reply in the sidebar, as on the phone.
- **View › Bigger ⌘+, Smaller ⌘−, Actual Size ⌘0** (`ViewZoom.swift`; the owner's yes of 28 October 2026, D-8): the whole window drawn larger, in steps of 115 % up to 150 %, kept on this Mac between launches. The page lays itself out in the window's size divided by the zoom and is drawn at the zoom (`.viewZoomed()` on the window's root and on the card over the page), so every measure grows together; it never lays itself out in less than the window's least size, 960 × 560, so a small window takes only as much zoom as leaves that room, and Bigger is dimmed while the window in front cannot take its next step (fix round 2). Every menu of the app — a right-click's, a select's list and the agent's "…" — stands in a window of its own (`FleetMenu`) and takes the zoom of the window it opens from (`zoomedAsItsWindow`). At 100 % it is the window as it is.
- The window opens over the whole visible screen at each start (`defaultWindowPlacement`, no restored frame). Every page but the Tasks board is one centered column of at most 1,100 pt with 24 pt margins, which `FleetPage` gives it (see One page, one scaffold); a width that changes from page to page moves the content sideways as the owner walks through the app. Within it a form page's header and cards take 680 pt from the column's left edge, and boards and tables its whole width. The Tasks board takes the window's whole width in the same margins (`wide:`, 2 November 2026); a conversation and a task thread keep the narrower reading measure of 780 pt.

## In the window, not on top of it

The owner's rule: no popovers and no modal sheets for normal views. A detail opens in the content area. One window over the page is permitted, because it is a control and not a view: the app's menu (`FleetMenu`), where every right-click menu and the list of every `FleetSelect` open. A detail that shows over the page, at the owner's request (16 October 2026), is always a `fleetModal`: one card in the middle of a backdrop that dims **the whole window** (black 0.69: the canvas to #080808, the sidebar to #0A0A0A; T-41), in a window of its own over the app's window. A click outside the card closes it, and so do Done and Esc. It makes no difference where in a page the modifier sits, which is the point: a modifier on a section used to dim that section alone and leave a black frame around it. The app has no `sheet`, because a sheet brings its own frame and its own colour. The app has **one second window**, Report a problem (`ReportProblemView.swift`, Help → Report a problem, 18 October 2026): a card over the page would cover the screen the customer is describing, and the report has to stand beside that screen while he writes about it. It has no bar at its top either: its band holds the window's buttons, and its title stands where every page's stands (the margins of 30 October 2026). The app has **one second window**, Report a problem (`ReportProblemView.swift`, Help → Report a problem, 18 October 2026): a card over the page would cover the screen the customer is describing, and the report has to stand beside that screen while he writes about it. It answers before it asks — "What Founderizer can see" lists only what is wrong (`FleetTrouble`: the hub, a machine gone quiet, a provider signed out, an update that failed, and each `verify` check that failed with the `fix` that check itself wrote), with a block to paste into his own agent (`TroublePrompt`) where the fix is work on a machine and not his own step, and a button that names the server's page it opens. A server with no agent is in it too (`GET /ui/servers`: `/ui/machines` has a row for each agent), by what its own page goes by: its checks, its own reading, a restart. **The cross of a server's Security row sends him here, so every server with a current reading has a row for what is off**, in that cross's words and with no line under them (`Firewall off · worker-3`, the owner's yes of 30 October 2026), and only its Open button: it goes when a check of a closer look that failed says the same thing with its fix. A fleet with nothing wrong gets one line and no list of the checks that passed. What those checks said is one of the facts the report carries, drawn in full in "Sent with it" before it goes: a value a row would cut off with an ellipsis is a value he did not read, so a long one takes a `FormBlock` and wraps.

**A block that can be absent cannot read what decides its own absence.** The owner's rule makes this common:
most sections of this app are there only when they have something to say. A SwiftUI view whose body resolves to
nothing installs none of its own modifiers — no `.task`, no `.onAppear` — so a section that reads the hub in its
own `.task` and hides itself when that read comes back empty never reads at all, and stays hidden for ever. The
read belongs to whatever decides the block exists: the page holds it and passes the answer down (`SettingsPage`
reads the channels, `ChannelsSettings` takes the snapshot it decided on). The same holds for a row inside a
card, and it bit the Channels row on 20 October 2026.

It never takes the keyboard from the window under it, as a macOS menu (only for VoiceOver does it take it, so that its rows can be read): the arrows, Return, Space, Esc and a long list's letters work while it is open, a key with ⌘ closes it and goes on to the window, and a click anywhere else, the wheel elsewhere and another app close it and go no further. A menu of actions opens with no row highlighted, a select's list with its selection highlighted, and an item that cannot act takes no highlight. A row is found under the pointer only where the rows show, so one scrolled under the filter's field is never chosen, and a choice goes to the page's newest action.

## Marks before words

Use a mark where it says the same as a word; keep the word in the hover text and in the accessibility label.

**Use as few words as possible, on every screen.** The owner, 20 October 2026, after cutting a card down to a
switch and a folder: "remember that, avoid as much text as possible everywhere!!" — and, the same day, about the
menus: "not even sentences, just a check or a cross, and on hover probably show the detail? This applies to all
menus, it needs to be simpler!". So, before a word is drawn:

- A **mark** instead of a word, and the word behind the mark (`StateMark`, `RowStateMark`).
- A row that only reports that things are **fine is cut**. A row is there because something needs him, or because
  it is a thing he sets.
- **No explanation under a control.** A sentence that says the same thing every day is read once and skipped for
  ever after, so it is not a warning, it is wallpaper. Say it where the decision is actually made — in the save
  panel where he names the file, in the sheet he is filling in, in the file the copy carries — or do not say it.
- **No page subtitle, no section sentence, no footer that explains the section.** A footer carries what went
  wrong, and nothing else.
- A **heading is not a sentence**: naming what a group of marks is a group of is organisation, not prose, and
  that is the one kind of word this rule asks for more of (`HealthView.connected`).

## Statuses must be true

- **Working** means that the hub reports a task in work now, or that the agent works on his newest chat message (its chat says "Thinking", `FleetAgent.thinks`). **A task in state working is work only while its session works on it** (`FleetTask.works`, hub 1.147; the owner, 13 November 2026: "the agent replied but the sidebar is still animating!"): a task that its agent parked until a time (`parked_until`), and one whose last turn ended with nothing of it running, move nothing and have no green of work; they keep the mark and the word of their state, at rest. A paused, queued, stalled or waiting task is not work; a task that waits for the owner makes the dot orange. **Online** says only that the agent's helper answers. The hub reports no other activity, so never write "idle".
- A chat message is answered only by a reply that names that exact message. A reply to an older message does not answer a newer one, and a later report without a link leaves it unknown. An agent card shows that state in a few words ("Reply not linked", "Read, no reply yet", "Message not read"); the full reason ("A later report has no link to your message") and the time of the message are in the hover text, for assistive apps.
- The account email is the provider account's own email, as the runtime reported it through the hub (feature `account_email`): `AccountEmail` is current (`FleetAgent.accountEmail(at:)` with a current hub state), an old reading ("Email reading is old", with the last value only as "Last reading"), or unknown ("Email not reported"). An agent's Settings shows the email of the agent's own route and, when it has one, of its desktop route. An owner's account label is a label; never show it as the email.
- Secrets is one list of the hub's catalog, logins and API keys together, sorted by name. A row has the kind as a mark, the name from the catalog (a login as "host · user name", an API key by its variable name in a monospaced font), and one line with the kind and the agents that have it; the list takes no second key of one variable name (the owner, 13 November 2026), and two keys of one name from before differ by those agents. On an agent's Can use the row ends in its `−` (Take back), or in nothing where a profile lists it, and the group's heading has the `+` (Give, `use`). A click on the row opens its detail: the name, the kind with a Copy control for the reference, and each agent with its access in a few words (No access, Can use it, Reads it in its sessions in orange; **each level has one name wherever it stands**, its control's: Uses it and Reads it, and in At most; the row says what the manager may give in those words, "Manager may give: Reads it") and its control: Give (`use`: its vault uses it, and the agent never sees it); for a login, Take back; for an API key, a `FleetSelect` with Uses it, Reads it (its subtitle is the warning: the key is in the agent's sessions, and the agent can copy it) and Take it back. An agent whose profile lists the reference has a check mark and no control: its profile changes that. After a grant, one line says when the agent's vault access changed or cannot change yet, and when a read starts. One line names the projects that have it in their scope. Under the agents stand the same two rows as in the detail of a repository or an MCP server, **Who may give it** and, while the manager may, At most (`StandingRows`, the owner's yes of 24 October 2026), with no heading of the same words over them and no footer while the manager may give it (D-2), and the row of a secret that the fleet manager may give says so, as theirs do ("Manager: up to read"). A secret that no agent, profile or project has shows Remove from Fleet: the reference leaves the catalog (`POST /ui/team/logins` with `delete`), and its value stays in the vaults. The detail does not show the masking in texts: it is the hub's own protection, and it needs no choice. The page has no section for older labels, for references without a name or for the logins that agents made: a made login shows when it is in the catalog.
- A login item of the setup check (a server's page) shows the catalog's name of its reference, or "Login without a name". The name is a label only and says nothing about access. The check's own state (Passed, Failed, Not verified) and "Required for activation" do not change. On demand, the row gives the vault's reason in ordinary words, the next step where the owner has one, and the reference (`SetupCheck.loginProbeWords`). `not_found` says that the vault's store has no item with that reference, and `no_access` comes only from an older vault, which opened another store. A login that the vault did not test is `not probed` and reads as not tested, never as refused. A reason that Fleet does not know gets a neutral sentence. The other check items show as before.
- **A machine with no connection on record** (`MachineAccessSection.swift`, hub feature `machine_access`) has one row in its details, and a machine that has one has nothing new: a cross with "Not on record" behind it, whose action opens the three fields — the user, the port and a Screen Sharing switch — and whose Record writes them to the fleet's own `team.json` (`POST /ui/team/access`, against the version the section read; on 409 it reads again and writes once more). The user is the one question: the port is 22 and Screen Sharing is off until he says otherwise, and a user the hub would refuse — one that is neither `root` nor the OS user that machine's agents run as — is said in the row's own warning before anything is sent. Whether the row exists at all is `MachineAccessForm.isAsked` in FleetCore, so one rule answers it. The connection fields of a server's page (`MachineConnectionFields`) are a different thing and stay: they are this Mac's own SSH target, key and all, kept in its defaults, which the hub never sees.
- A capability item of the machine check that failed and that the hub does not require (`required` false) shows "Not available" in the secondary color with a neutral mark: the agent's settings do not use it, so the owner has nothing to do. Its detail gives the machine's reason on demand. A failed capability that the hub requires stays "Failed" with "Required for activation", and each other failed item stays "Failed". The app uses the hub's flag and does not read the capability list for this.
- **A task thread is what the agent said** (`RuntimeActivity.swift`, owner direction, 18 October 2026: "messages are so confusing… I dont like showing so much"). The rows are its messages, the progress notes it reported, the owner's guidance, an error, a permission that waits or was refused — and between two of them, one `FoldRow` that says how many steps the work took ("14 steps"; the running step's own title and "Running" while it runs; "2 failed" in orange). The steps open from that row, unchanged. Three kinds of row are not in the thread at all: a **Fleet tool call** (`fleet: report_progress`, `deliver_result`, `read_messages`, …), because Fleet already draws what each one did and the call was the same fact twice — the five that reach outside the fleet (`sign_in`, `hand_over`, `create_login`, `save_key`, `call_api`) are real actions and stay steps; a **permission the runtime allowed**, which says only that work the thread already shows was allowed to run; and a **thought**, which is the model talking to itself and goes in with the steps. A plan shows only while the task runs. The notes and permissions come from the hub's state and take their place by the time it stamped on them, so a note written in the middle of a run splits that run in two.
- Codex runs its tools from a script (`exec`), and the hub records the script and each call that the script made. The steps of a thread are the calls. A script that completed takes the place of its first call: the row shows the call with the call's own state and gives, on demand, the script line and the script's own result. The row keeps the script's place and identity, so an open detail stays open. The calls of a script are only the rows that the hub recorded between the script's first event and its last update (`ActivityRow.lastEventID`), and each of them must be a call with a final state. A script stays a "Run tools" row when it failed, when its result does not start with "Script completed", when it has not ended, when it has no call, when another script was open at the same time, and when it was already on screen with one of its calls. Thus no row goes away while events arrive, and a failed call, a stopped script and each independent call stay visible.
- **A screen reads the hub when something changed, and otherwise on its clock** (the owner wants every result on his screen at once). The fleet's own state (`/ui/state`) is read every three seconds, counted from the last read whoever ran it, and at once when the hub's change signal (`live_changes`, `GET /ui/wait`) says that something he sees changed: at most one such read in two seconds, the changes inside them folded into one at their end. A read that is asked for while one is on its way runs once more and is never dropped (`RunAgain`). A loop that reads its own route waits with `store.nap(seconds)` and never with `Task.sleep`: the nap ends at its interval, or at the first wake after it began, which is a change the hub announced (the same cap) or the answer to any write of this Mac, through any client of the hub (`HubWrites`, no cap: it is his own action). So the interval a page names is how old its reading may get when nothing wakes it, and not how soon a change shows. A timer that does not read the hub keeps its sleep: the Saved mark, a debounce, a hover delay, the desktop's frame loop, the local installer, the four-hour update check. **None of it is on the screen**: a wait that fails sets no connection error and no state, and tries again after 1 to 30 seconds. A hub without `live_changes` is read on the clocks alone, and a write still wakes the pages.
- A page that reads the hub again and again keeps four states apart (`RuleReading` and `ProfileReading`, both in `FleetCore/Readings.swift`): loading, current, an old reading, and failed. Without a current hub state every reading is old. Never show a failed read as an empty list, and never show an old reading as current: mark it, give no next time from it, and say that Fleet reads again. A reading that Founderizer is reading again is not old until that read fails or takes more than one second. **While the app runs** (fix round 3b, item 17), a read of the fleet's state on its way marks the reading old after eight seconds (`FleetStore.readPatience`; it was one second, and on 10 November 2026 a read of 2 to 7 s under load put the old-reading notice and every page's last-reading look on his screen for the rest of that read, about once in two minutes) — after twice its last read's time on a hub whose reads take longer, so a slow hub's reading does not turn old for part of every read — and it stays old until a read answers, its notice with it. **At a launch the same eight seconds** (`StateReading` in FleetCore, with its tests): the reading that this Mac kept from its last launch is current while the launch's first read is on its way, and old as soon as that read fails or has taken eight seconds (it was one second: a first read of 1.4 s put the old-reading notice over the page from 1.2 to 1.6 s after the window opened and took the agents' dots from the sidebar, three launches in three, and one read of his fleet in ten takes more than a second); one read that fails at once says nothing (the owner's "never for one short break"), and three in a row are the hub's own notice.
- **A failed or an old reading says why where the hub said it.** Both of those states carry the hub's own sentence (`HubError.said`), and both report one line built by `FleetReading.line(reason, instead:then:)`: the hub's sentence first where it wrote one, else the screen's own words for the same fact, and then what the screen must say whatever the cause — that the rows can be old, and the retry. The rest is never dropped, so a hub sentence can never make an old reading read as a current one. A page that keeps a last reading therefore reads with `FleetReading.read` and not with `try?`, which keeps the value and throws the reason away (`AgentPage.poll`, `TeamModel.read`).

## Saving

- An agent's Settings and Behaviour have no Save button: each control saves itself. A select or a checkbox saves at once; a text field when it loses focus or on Return (the full model name); a rule of Behaviour after 2 seconds without typing, and on Done, Back or another page. Never on each keystroke. The `SaveMark` beside the control says Saving…, Saved or Try again. A save waits behind a request that is on its way, and never behind one that waits in the request line (refused, or without an answer): it stops with Try again at once (`store.save(_:queue:)` with queue false), where it used to say Saving… until the owner discarded that request. There is no Save & continue and no draft footer.
- These saves go one at a time through the store, and a newer value of a field that waits takes the place of the older one. The model and the reasoning go in one save. When the hub answers 409 because `team.json` or the profile changed elsewhere, Fleet reads it again and sends only that field again with a new request ID; when the same field changed elsewhere, the control shows the new value. A pending command does the same in the store (`FleetStore.rebase`): a 409 is not a final refusal, so Fleet reads the base again and, when it really moved and the command is a state action or a write of one field, sends that same command against the fresh base once, with a new request ID. A whole form the owner typed against the version he read is never rebased: it stays the conflict his editor draws. Activate, Deactivate and Check server stay buttons.
- Every other command with several fields keeps its explicit Save or Create button: a project, a specialist, an account label, the reporting lines. A goal, a duty and a schedule are **not** among them any more (19 October 2026): the app reads all three and writes none, because the owner says what he wants in the chat and the agents write it down (`update_goal`, `set_duty`, `save_trigger`). Their pages keep only their state actions. Never send these to the hub on each keystroke, on focus loss or on a timer, and keep their conflict text ("Use current version"), pending-request line, Try again and Discard paths.
- Do not add confirmation dialogs. Put a destructive button at the end of its row group or button row.
- A grant that the hub commits at once is one click: in a secret's detail, Give gives the secret to an agent and Take back takes it back, and one line in the detail says when the agent's vault access changed or cannot change yet. A permission that the hub takes with a record (a project's permission for new logins) is a checkbox in that record's form and goes with its Save. Each such permission is off until the owner turns it on.
